The $0 AI Stack That Runs a One-Person Business in 2026

The solo operators we interviewed do $8K-$40K a month on under $60 of AI software. Here's the exact free-tier stack, role by role — and the honest math behind it.

MKBy Mara Kessler·Jul 18, 2026·15 min read

Here's a number that should make you sit up: the solo operators we interviewed for this piece run businesses doing $8K to $40K a month — and their total AI software spend is under $60. Some of them spend $0.

This isn't a "top 50 tools" listicle. It's the actual stack, role by role, that a one-person business can run on in 2026 without touching a credit card. Every tool below has a free tier that's genuinely usable, not a crippled demo.

The rules we used to build this stack

Three rules kept us honest. One: every tool must have a free tier that lets you do real work, not a 7-day tease. Two: it must replace something you'd otherwise pay a human or an expensive SaaS for. Three: it must save at least 3 hours a week — below that, the switching cost isn't worth it.

The biggest mistake solo operators make isn't picking the wrong tool — it's paying for five tools that overlap. Pick one per job. Master it. Move on.

The $0 stack, role by role

1Claude / ChatGPT (free tiers)$0

The job: Your strategist, copywriter, and analyst

One generalist assistant covers 70% of your AI needs: drafting emails and proposals, analyzing a spreadsheet you paste in, sanity-checking a pricing decision, writing your About page. Use both free tiers and rotate when one hits its limit — that's the whole trick.

2Canva (free)$0

The job: Your designer

Social graphics, pitch decks, invoices with your logo, product mockups. Canva's free tier is absurdly generous, and its Magic Studio AI tools (background remover, Magic Write) handle the jobs you'd otherwise send to a freelancer.

3Notion (free)$0

The job: Your operations brain

Client CRM, project tracker, SOPs, content calendar — all in one free workspace. The AI features are paid, but you don't need them: paste sections into your free assistant when you need a second brain.

4CapCut (free)$0

The job: Your video editor

Auto-captions that are actually accurate, background removal, and AI script-to-video for the shorts and reels that sell your service. The free version exports without a watermark for most uses — check current terms, they shift.

5Google Workspace free + Gmail AI$0

The job: Your front office

Gmail's built-in AI drafting and the free Docs/Sheets/Meet stack cover scheduling, proposals, and client calls. Not glamorous, but it's $0 and it never breaks — the most underrated combo in this whole list.

6Wave / Zoho Invoice (free)$0

The job: Your bookkeeper

Free invoicing and basic bookkeeping that looks professional and chases payments for you. Pair it with your free assistant to categorize expenses at month-end in ten minutes instead of an evening.

That's not a typo. The gap between "how a business ran in 2019" and "how a solo operator runs in 2026" is roughly $8,000 a year — and the solo operator often ships faster.

When to actually start paying

Pay for a tool only when its free tier becomes a provable bottleneck — you can name the specific deal or hour it cost you. For most people that moment comes with the assistant (upgrade to $20/mo when rate limits block real work) and Canva (when you need brand kits). Everything else stays free longer than you'd expect.

FAQ

Is a $0 stack really enough to look professional?
Yes — clients judge your output, not your tools. A Canva-designed proposal from a free Gmail looks identical to one built on $500/mo of SaaS. The professionalism comes from you, the leverage comes from the stack.
What's the single highest-ROI tool on this list?
The free assistant (Claude or ChatGPT). It touches every other role — writing, analysis, planning — so the hour savings compound across your whole week.
When does the free-tier juggling stop being worth it?
When you're spending more than 30 minutes a week working around limits. At that point the $20/mo upgrade has already paid for itself — that's the whole point of the bottleneck rule.
