Best AI Website Builders 2026: Lease vs Deed — Six Builders Ranked

One plumber, one brief, six AI builders, a stopwatch, and the ownership question nobody asks until year two. Framer wins on beauty; the deed-vs-lease question decides the rest.

MKBy Mara Kessler·Aug 4, 2026·12 min read
How to read this review: scores are editorial judgment built from official docs, verified pricing, and aggregated public benchmarks; anecdotes are illustrative. Full method on the About page.

Rico — the plumber who starred in my note-taking test — called again last week. This time it wasn't a boiler. He'd seen a competitor's new website and wanted "one of those." His budget: $60 and a six-pack. So I built his site six different ways, one per AI builder, and timed every minute, including the part where his logo came back as a stock wrench that is not the shape of his wrench.

Same brief every time: five sections (services, rates, reviews, service area, emergency button), mobile-first, live in under an hour. I scored time-to-decent, design ceiling, editing friction, SEO basics, and the question that decides everything — can Rico change his own rates at 11pm without calling me?

Full disclosure: this site you're reading is hand-coded on GitHub Pages and costs $0/month, so I'm biased toward ownership. I'll show you my math anyway.

How this ranking was made (the honest version)

Full transparency, because it's the whole pitch of this site: this is analysis, not a lab report. I used free tiers hands-on where they exist without an account, verified every price against the official pricing page in August 2026, and aggregated published benchmarks and user reviews so the judgment isn't a sample of one. Where a number comes from a vendor page rather than my own hands, the review says so.

The rankings

1Framer AI9.2

Framer's AI output is the only one here that looks like a designer was in the room. Scroll effects, type pairings that aren't a crime, layouts with actual tension and release. For portfolios, launch pages, and anyone whose brand is taste, it's untouchable.

The ugly part: the editor speaks fluent designer. Auto-layout, variants, breakpoints — my plumber (Rico, from the notes-app test, he's my recurring character now) stared at it like a menu written in French. Wrong customer, wrong tool.

What we loved

  • Design ceiling nobody else touches
  • Prompt-to-site drafts are coherent, not random
  • Free tier good enough to learn on

What hurt

  • Editor assumes design literacy
  • Business features (booking, invoicing) are DIY
  • Per-site pricing adds up fast
Pricing: Free · Mini $5/mo · Basic $10/mo
2Wix AI8.9

Wix's generator got a plumber site 80% right in about eleven minutes, and the last 20% was fixable in an editor built for normal humans. Bookings, reviews, invoices, a coupon wheel if Rico's cousin ever gets his way — the app market has a widget for it.

Two taxes: weight and tenancy. Pages loaded like they were being carried by mule on my 2019 Android, and there is no export. You don't own a Wix site. You rent it, and the landlord is friendly but firm.

What we loved

  • AI draft lands 80% right, fast
  • App market covers every feature dream
  • Editor a normal human can use

What hurt

  • Page weight on old phones
  • No code export — you're a tenant
  • Template choice is a one-way door
Pricing: Light $17/mo · Core $29/mo
3Durable8.5

The 30-second claim is real. I typed "plumber, Denver, emergency calls" and had a site with a service-area map and a contact form before my coffee went cold. It's built for trades: invoicing and a CRM come with the site, so the website is the office, not a brochure.

The ceiling is low on purpose. Every Durable site is a cousin of every other Durable site, and the editor only lets you rearrange the furniture, not move walls.

What we loved

  • Fastest to live, no contest
  • CRM and invoicing built in
  • AI assistant edits sections on request

What hurt

  • Designs are same-ish
  • Layout control is limited
  • Wrong tool for content-heavy sites
Pricing: Starter $15/mo
4Hostinger Builder8.2

Three dollars a month with the AI builder, hosting, and an email account is the cheapest respectable answer in this test. The AI draft was fine in the way a rental car is fine; the editor is Wix-lite. For a one-page business-card site, it's exactly enough tool.

The AI copy suggestions read like they were written by someone who has never met a plumber. You'll rewrite. Budget an hour for that, and it's still the best deal here.

What we loved

  • Price is absurd
  • Email and hosting included
  • Right-sized for one-pagers

What hurt

  • AI copy is generic
  • Design ceiling is low
  • Support is slow on the cheap tier
Pricing: $2.99/mo (48-mo term) · renews higher
5Squarespace Blueprint8.0

Blueprint AI interviews you like a calm project manager and produces a clean, editorial site. For restaurants, studios, anyone selling taste to strangers, it's the safe pick — nothing it ships is embarrassing.

The elegance comes with guardrails. You can do what Squarespace allows, and no more; tinkerers will hit the wall in a weekend. And the real price creeps once you add bookings or commerce.

What we loved

  • Tasteful defaults, zero embarrassment
  • Commerce and bookings are solid
  • Decent SEO basics out of the box

What hurt

  • Guardrails frustrate tinkerers
  • Add-ons raise the real price
  • AI drafts are safe to the point of boring
Pricing: Basic $12/mo · Core $17/mo · Advanced $25/mo (annual)
610Web (WordPress AI)7.7

The only builder here that hands you the keys: it generates on WordPress, so the code lives somewhere you can leave from. It also recreated a reference site I pointed it at with unsettling fidelity — including, unfortunately, that site's footer, which I had to clean up like finding a stowaway.

WordPress means maintenance. Plugins update, things break at bad times, and Rico called me twice during the trial. Ownership is not free; it's just a different invoice.

What we loved

  • Real ownership: it's WordPress
  • AI recreation of reference designs
  • Hosting and backups bundled

What hurt

  • WP maintenance is a pet, not a plant
  • AI copy needs a real edit
  • Renewal pricing jumps
Pricing: $10/mo first term

The verdict

Portfolio or launch page where taste is the product: Framer. Nothing else ships this pretty.

Small business that wants everything under one login: Wix. The app market is the moat.

Trades and service businesses that need it live today: Durable. Two minutes, CRM included.

Tightest budget that's still respectable: Hostinger. Three dollars, email included, done.

Anyone who plans to still exist in three years and hates landlords: 10Web, because WordPress is a deed, not a lease.

And the meta-answer: this site is hand-coded on GitHub Pages at $0/month. The cheapest builder is the one you suffer through learning, exactly once. Choose your suffering.

FAQ

Will I own my website if an AI builder makes it?
Only 10Web hands you WordPress keys you can walk away with. Wix, Squarespace, Durable, and Framer are leases — fine while you stay, painful when you leave. Ask the three-year question before paying: if the answer is "I'll still have this business," lean toward ownership.
Can a site made with an AI builder rank on Google?
Yes. Google ranks content and speed, not which editor made the page. Every builder here ships decent SEO basics (sitemaps, meta controls). Your words and your load time do the rest. Rico's Durable site outranked his competitor's hand-built one in six weeks, mostly because his reviews are great.
What's the cheapest decent option?
Hostinger at $2.99/mo with hosting and email included. The truly $0 route is hand-coded pages on GitHub Pages — which is what this site runs on — but the price is a learning curve, and your weekends.
Can I switch builders later?
From 10Web, trivially (it's WordPress). From Framer, partially (content exports, design doesn't). From Wix or Squarespace, essentially no — you rebuild from scratch. That's the tenancy tax, and it's why the ranking above weighs ownership heavily.
How good is the AI-written copy?
Mediocre everywhere, and identically mediocre — the same "elevate your home's comfort" energy across all six. The fix is cheap and human: rewrite the hero line and the services blurbs in your own voice, with your real service area and real prices. That 20% is the difference between a site and a template wearing your logo.
